# Orange Condition Classification Report

## Objective

Classify orange images into the source dataset labels `blackspot`, `canker`,
`fresh`, and `grenning`. This is an orange-condition classification task, not
avocado ripeness classification.

## Dataset understanding and preparation

The supplied dataset contains 1,090 images in its original train/test folders.
The original test split is kept separate for final evaluation. A stratified
validation set is drawn from the 991 training images, producing 842 training,
149 validation, and 99 test samples.

| Class | Train | Test | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `blackspot` | 184 | 22 | 206 |
| `canker` | 179 | 22 | 201 |
| `fresh` | 281 | 33 | 314 |
| `grenning` | 347 | 22 | 369 |
| **Total** | **991** | **99** | **1,090** |

The EDA notebook found no unreadable images. `blackspot`, `canker`, and
`grenning` images are 800x800 JPGs, while `fresh` images are variable-size PNGs.
This format/resolution difference may act as a shortcut, so the high scores
should not be assumed to generalize to images from other sources.

## Methods

The classical baseline combines normalized HSV and CIELAB color histograms
with a Local Binary Pattern texture histogram and a class-balanced RBF SVM.

The deep-learning approach replaces the classifier head of ImageNet-pretrained
MobileNetV2 and freezes the feature backbone. Training uses horizontal flips,
small rotations, and mild brightness/contrast changes; early stopping monitors
validation loss. The model was trained for 12 epochs, reached 99.3% validation
accuracy at the final epoch, and achieved its lowest validation loss
(approximately 0.0904) at epoch 12.

## Held-out test results

Both models were evaluated on the same 99 supplied test images. The reported
metrics were generated by `notebooks/04_model_evaluation_and_demo.ipynb` and
are saved in `test_metrics.json` and `test_predictions.csv`.

| Model | Test accuracy | Macro precision | Macro recall | Macro F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HSV/LAB + LBP + SVM | 96.97% | 97.00% | 96.59% | 96.57% |
| MobileNetV2 | 94.95% | 94.61% | 94.32% | 94.45% |

The SVM correctly classified 96 of 99 images. Its only errors were three
`canker` images predicted as `blackspot`. MobileNetV2 correctly classified 94
of 99 images: two `blackspot` images and two `canker` images were predicted as
the other diseased class, and one `canker` image was predicted as `fresh`.
Both models classified all `fresh` and `grenning` test examples correctly
except for the one MobileNetV2 `canker`-to-`fresh` error. The confusion-matrix
plot is saved at `figures/confusion_matrices.png`.

On this split, the classical baseline performed better than MobileNetV2 by
2.02 percentage points in accuracy and 2.13 percentage points in macro F1.
This is a result on a small, potentially shortcut-prone test set, not evidence
that the SVM will outperform the neural model on a new domain.

## Assignment questions

### 1. How does the model perform in production?

Production performance has not been measured. A CPU-only, batch-size-one,
inference-only benchmark for MobileNetV2 on a 224x224 tensor measured a mean
of 20.17 ms, median of 20.35 ms, and 95th percentile of 30.34 ms across 30
timed runs after five warmups. This excludes image decoding and resizing and
is specific to the machine used for this run; it is not end-to-end production
latency. Evaluate on the intended hardware, camera, lighting, and an external
held-out dataset before making production claims.

### 2. What are the drawbacks?

- The test split is small (22–33 examples per class), so metrics have
  substantial sampling uncertainty.
- Class counts are imbalanced; `grenning` has the most samples.
- Image format and resolution correlate with the `fresh` class and may let
  models learn dataset-specific cues.
- Lighting, shadows, background, camera, and framing can cause distribution
  shifts or misleading visual cues.
- Labels may be ambiguous, and RGB images cannot measure internal fruit
  quality.

### 3. What would be improved with more time?

- Collect larger, balanced data across cultivars, farms, cameras, and lighting.
- Obtain verified labels and evaluate on an external test set.
- Compare additional augmentations and fine-tune selected backbone layers,
  selecting changes using validation data only.
- Measure end-to-end latency and evaluate deployment optimizations on target
  hardware.
- Explore additional sensors if the application requires internal quality
  estimation.
